Create an editable 15-slide presentation for a business audience that explains JPMorgan Chase's third-quarter 2021 earnings to readers who are not finance specialists.

## Story to tell

Show why the reported $11.7 billion net income and $3.74 diluted EPS need context: the quarter included a $2.1 billion net credit reserve release (+$0.52 EPS) and a $566 million tax benefit (+$0.19 EPS). The release reports net income excluding significant items of $9.6 billion and EPS of $3.03. Explain how the bank's four businesses contributed, then close with capital, customer activity, community support, and the stated forward-looking caveat.

## Required coverage

- Cover with company, quarter, and reporting date.
- A concise executive takeaway, followed by reported results and the significant-item context.
- Firmwide revenue and income statement comparisons with periods and units.
- Credit reserve releases and their stated EPS effect; clearly distinguish management's non-GAAP measures from reported GAAP results.
- Consumer & Community Banking, Corporate & Investment Bank, Commercial Banking, and Asset & Wealth Management, with the supplied year-over-year measures.
- Capital, shareholder distributions, branch/digital expansion, and community credit/capital activity.
- Neutral forward-looking statement summary and the press-release contacts/conference-call information.

## Accuracy and sourcing

Use only `material.txt`. Keep every number exactly as supplied, including units, comparison periods, signs, and GAAP/non-GAAP labels. Do not recompute growth rates or add external facts. Cite the earnings-release section in the bottom-left of every slide containing sourced figures. Do not invent conclusions or imply that reserve releases are recurring earnings.

## Presentation quality

Build a clear narrative rather than a topic dump. Use a consistent, restrained financial identity with a deliberate title/body type scale, strong alignment, and ample whitespace. Vary compositions where the content changes: include at least three editable native charts for meaningful comparisons, a bridge or other clear native diagram for the significant-item context, and typography or simple diagrams for qualitative material. Keep charts fully labeled with units, periods, and legends. Avoid repeating a card grid as the default layout. Keep body copy concise and readable when projected. Include at least five slides with specific quantitative details.

Deliver exactly 15 slides as editable PowerPoint objects. The requested audience, source facts, and clear visual communication take priority over generic benchmark wording that does not match this financial-results case.
