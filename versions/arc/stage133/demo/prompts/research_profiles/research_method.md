# Research focus: research method

Investigate whether the research approach can answer its question. Examine
question formulation, evidence selection, measurement, experiment design, and
evaluation. Check for confounding, circular validation, missing counterexamples,
and claims stronger than the observations.

Check that concrete starting material and actual evidence/execution access support
an attainable intermediate objective and a locally discriminating evaluation.
Identify the reachable stage/input, observable expected result, executor and
cost/stop bound; narrow or request prerequisites when these are missing. Study
cross-phase successes, failures and the agent's repeated reasoning where granted.
Propose a method or representation suited to the bottleneck, including ideas from
prior knowledge, then distinguish candidate rationale from local test evidence.
Name the next applicable use and feedback needed to revise the method.

Report weaknesses supported by the record, useful methodological revisions, and
how their effect could be evaluated. Preserve negative or inconclusive results;
agreement between investigators is not independent validation.
