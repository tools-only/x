Q3-21 Financial Highlights
The quarter at a glance: stronger profits, record balances, solid capital

What happened at the company level

  • Net income rose 58% to $7.7 billion, or $0.85 per diluted share — a big lift versus Q3-20, when the bank booked a much larger provision for credit losses.
  • Revenue, net of interest expense, increased 12% to $22.8 billion: net interest income (NII)B up $1 billion, or 10%, to $11.1 billion (strong deposit growth and related investment of liquidity, plus Paycheck Protection Program activity); noninterest income up 14% to $11.7 billion (record asset management fees, strong investment banking revenue, higher sales & trading).
  • Provision for credit losses improved by $2.0 billion to a benefit of $624 million, reflecting a reserve release of $1.1 billion driven primarily by asset quality improvements.C
  • Noninterest expense was relatively flat at $14.4 billion — higher revenue-related expenses largely offset by lower litigation and lower COVID-related costs.
  • Average loans in business segments up $14 billion QoQ to $903 billion; excluding PPP, up $21 billion QoQ.D Average deposits up $247 billion, or 15%, to $1.9 trillion.
  • Average Global Liquidity Sources rose $261 billion, or 30%, to a record $1.1 trillion.E CET1 ratio 11.1% (Standardized); returned $11.7 billion to shareholders via dividends and buybacks.F
Plain English "Revenue, net of interest expense" = interest earned minus interest paid on deposits, plus all fee and trading income. A "provision benefit" means the bank judged it no longer needed as much money set aside for possible loan losses, which flows back into profit.

What is "All Other"? All Other primarily consists of asset and liability management (ALM) activities, liquidating businesses and certain expenses not otherwise allocated to a business segment. ALM activities manage interest-rate and foreign-currency risk for the whole bank, and substantially all of their results are allocated to the business segments. That is why revenue is negative (mainly funding costs) while the bottom line swings with tax items.
  • Net loss of $54 million, vs. net income of $1.9B in Q2-21 and $298M in Q3-20 — Q2-21 and Q3-20 each included positive income tax adjustments from the revaluation of U.K. deferred tax assets of $2.0 billion and $0.7 billion, respectively.
  • Revenue down modestly; included higher partnership losses for Environmental, Social and Governance (ESG) investments (offset in All Other tax expense).
  • Noninterest expense declined 37%, driven primarily by lower litigation expense.

Reading the swing, quarter by quarter

  • Q2-21's $1.9B included the $2.0B U.K. deferred tax asset revaluation benefit — a one-time accounting gain, not operating earnings.
  • Q3-21 corporate effective tax rate (ETR) was 14%; excluding ESG tax credits, the ETR would have been approximately 25%.
  • Bottom line for the full company: All Other's small loss was more than offset by the four business segments' combined results.

Plain English The "provision" is the quarterly earnings charge that tops up the bank's loss reserve; a negative provision (a benefit) means losses were smaller than expected, so the bank gave part of the reserve back to earnings. The allowance ratio shows how many cents of reserve back each dollar of loans — 1.43 cents… no: 1.43% means about $0.014 of reserve per loan dollar, well above the 0.20% rate loans are actually being charged off.

Caveats and conventions: how to read this deck responsibly

Presentation conventions

  • Comparisons are to the year-ago quarter (Q3-20) unless noted1 — most growth figures in this deck are year over year, not quarter over quarter.
  • Loan and deposit balances are shown on an average basis unless noted1; "EOP" items are at end of period.
  • Segments and All Other are reported on a fully taxable-equivalent (FTE) basis1,2 — tax-exempt income is adjusted upward to make it comparable with taxable income.
  • Non-GAAP measures used here: pretax, pre-provision income (PTPI)(H); NII on an FTE basis(B); Global Markets revenue and net income excluding net DVA(G); tangible common equity, tangible book value and related ratios (p.9, note 1; reconciliations on p.19). GAAP reconciliations appear in the release (p.19).
  • Credit ratios do not include loans accounted for under the fair value option (p.8); charge-off ratios are annualized for quarterly presentation (p.14).
  • ROAC is a management measure: net income adjusted for cost of funds, earnings credits and certain intangible-related expenses, ÷ average allocated capital; other companies may calculate it differently. n/m = not meaningful (p.16).

Specific caveats to keep in mind

  • Regulatory capital ratios at September 30, 2021 are preliminary(F) and reported under both Standardized and Advanced approaches; the lower (Standardized for CET1) is used to assess adequacy.
  • All Other's results swing with one-off tax items: Q2-21 and Q3-20 included positive income tax adjustments from the revaluation of U.K. deferred tax assets of $2.0B and $0.7B, respectively (p.7). Q3-21 corporate effective tax rate was 14% (~25% excluding ESG tax credits).
  • Q2-21 company net income of $9.2B included a $1.2B income tax benefit and a larger provision benefit (p.2) — useful context when comparing quarters.
  • Current-period information is preliminary and based on company data available at the time of the presentation (p.14).
  • Certain prior-period amounts have been reclassified among the segments to conform to current-period presentation (p.16).
  • Third-party rankings (J.D. Power, Dealogic, Euromoney, etc.) are as cited in the release; see page 11 for the full list of business-leadership sources.
