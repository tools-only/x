Capital, Liquidity and Loss Rates — Three Healthy Trends

CET1 ratio — “Well Capitalized”
Avg. Global Liquidity Sources ($B) — “Strong Liquidity”
Net charge-off ratio — “Low Loss Rates”
“We grew revenues faster than expenses, producing year-over-year operating leverage in every business segment and 12% for the company… Asset quality remained strong, with loss rates approaching 50-year lows, enabling the release of loan loss reserves again this quarter. … we were able to increase the quarterly dividend by 17% and buy back nearly $10 billion in common stock.”
— Paul Donofrio, Chief Financial Officer (p. 2)
What this means CET1 is the bank’s top-shelf cushion of high-quality capital: 11.1% is comfortably above the 9.5% minimum, so it still has room to pay dividends and buy back stock. Global Liquidity Sources are cash and high-quality liquid securities ready to meet funding needs — a record $1.1T. The charge-off ratio is the share of loans that are never repaid: 0.20% is near 50-year lows.

Why the quarter looks odd

  • Net loss of $54M, vs. net income of $1.9B in Q2-21 and $298M in Q3-20
  • Q2-21 and Q3-20 each included positive one-time income tax adjustments from revaluing U.K. deferred tax assets of $2.0B and $0.7B — that is why Q2-21 looks so profitable
  • Revenue down modestly; included higher partnership losses for ESG investments (offset in All Other tax expense)
  • Noninterest expense down 37%, driven primarily by lower litigation expense
  • Q3-21 total corporate effective tax rate was 14%; excluding ESG tax credits it would have been ~25%
What this means “All Other” is where the bank parks items that don’t belong to a customer-facing segment — mostly risk-management hedging activity and unallocated costs. Its swings are largely one-time tax and litigation items, so this line is not comparable across quarters and should not be read as a business that is growing or shrinking.

What moved

  • Net charge-offs fell $132M, or 22%, QoQ to $463M — Consumer down $184M to $329M on lower card losses; Commercial low at $134M; ratio down 7 bps to 0.20%
  • Provision was a $624M benefit — a net $1.1B reserve release on asset quality improvements (Consumer $0.2B; Commercial $0.8B)
  • Allowance for credit losses (incl. unfunded commitments) down 7% QoQ to $14.7B; allowance for loan & lease losses down $0.9B, or 7%, to $13.2B (1.43% of loans)
  • Nonperforming loans down $193M QoQ to $4.7B, primarily Commercial; commercial reservable criticized utilized exposure down $4.7B to $24.1B across broad industry improvements
What this means The provision is the bank’s estimate of expected loan losses — like a rainy-day fund for bad loans. Because actual losses (charge-offs) were far below plan, the bank took $1.1B out of the fund, which is a gain. Nonperforming loans are ones the borrower has largely stopped paying; those are falling too. Lower ratios mean the loan book is healthier than at any point in roughly half a century.
