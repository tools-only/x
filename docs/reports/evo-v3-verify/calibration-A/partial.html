BANK OF AMERICA ■
Third-Quarter 2021 Earnings, Explained

A record-deposit quarter: $7.7 billion net income as the economy reopened

Bank of America reported Q3-21 net income of $7.7 billion, or $0.85 per diluted share — up 58% from a year ago — as deposit growth reached $1.9 trillion on average, loan balances rose for a second straight quarter, and credit losses continued to improve.

Earnings Overview
  • Net income rose 58% to $7.7 billion, or $0.85 per diluted share (vs. $4.9 billion / $0.51 in Q3-20).
  • Revenue, net of interest expense, increased 12% to $22.8 billion:
    • Net interest income (NII)(B) up $1 billion, or 10%, to $11.1 billion — driven by strong deposit growth and related investment of liquidity, and Paycheck Protection Program (PPP) activities.
    • Noninterest income up 14% to $11.7 billion — record asset management fees, strong investment banking revenue, higher sales & trading revenues.
  • Provision for credit losses improved by $2.0 billion to a benefit of $624 million — a reserve release of $1.1 billion driven primarily by asset-quality improvements(C).
  • Noninterest expense relatively flat at $14.4 billion — higher revenue-related expenses largely offset by lower litigation expense and lower COVID-related costs.
  • Balance sheet momentum:
    • Average business-segment loans and leases up $14 billion QoQ to $903 billion; excluding PPP, up $21 billion QoQ(D).
    • Average deposits up $247 billion, or 15%, to $1.9 trillion.
    • Average Global Liquidity Sources up $261 billion, or 30%, to a record $1.1 trillion(E).
  • Capital: CET1 ratio 11.1% (Standardized); returned $11.7 billion to shareholders through common stock dividends and share repurchases(F).

Net income by quarter ($ in billions)

Q2-21 was the highest; Q3-21 remains up sharply from the year-ago quarter. (Source page 2)

Why it matters for a non-specialist

  • Deposits are the bank's main funding source: more deposits → more to lend and invest → higher net interest income.
  • The provision swung to a benefit, meaning the bank judged it had set aside more than needed for expected loan losses and released some of it back into profit.

Earnings Overview

Consumer Banking

  • Net income of $3.0 billion.
  • Deposit balances exceeded $1.0 trillion for the first time, up 16%.
  • Consumer investment assets up $87 billion, or 32%, to a record $353 billion, driven by market valuations and strong client flows of $21 billion since Q3-20.
  • Accelerated client activity:
    • Record consumer checking accounts: 34.2 million; 93% primary3
    • Combined credit and debit card spend up 21% to $201 billion (credit +26%, debit +17%)
    • 3.2 million consumer investment accounts, up 9%

Global Wealth & Investment Management (GWIM)

  • Record net income of $1.2 billion.
  • Record client balances of $3.7 trillion, up $626 billion, or 20%, driven by higher market valuations and $91 billion in client flows since Q3-20.
  • Deposits up 16% to $339 billion; pretax margin of 31%.
  • Accelerated client activity:
    • Record AUM balances of $1.6 trillion, up 23%
    • Average loan balances up 8% to $200 billion — 46th consecutive quarter of growth
    • Merrill Lynch Wealth Management added ~4,200 net new households; Private Bank added ~275 net new relationships

Global Banking

  • Net income of $2.5 billion.
  • Total investment banking fees (excl. self-led) increased 23% to near-record levels of $2.2 billion — record advisory fees of $654 million, up 65%.
  • Deposits up 13% to $534 billion.
  • Accelerated client activity:
    • Debt underwriting fees rose 26%; 9 of the top 10 debt deals4
    • Raised $221 billion in capital on behalf of clients in Q3-21; $728 billion year-to-date5

Global Markets

  • Record net income of $926 million.
  • Sales and trading revenue up 12% to $3.6 billion, including net debit valuation adjustment (DVA) losses of $20 million: FICC $2.0 billion, Equities $1.6 billion.
  • Excluding net DVA, sales and trading revenue up 9% to $3.6 billion; FICC down 5% to $2.0 billion(G); Equities up 33% to $1.6 billion(G).
  • Accelerated client activity:
    • Average assets increased $124 billion to $805 billion, driven by higher client balances in equities and loan growth

Reading the rows, in plain terms

  • Revenue, net of interest expense = net interest income (the spread earned on loans vs. deposits) plus fees and trading income.
  • Provision for credit losses = the charge set aside for expected loan losses. A negative number means reserves were released, boosting income.
  • PTPI strips out the provision, isolating earnings power before credit-cycle effects (non-GAAP).
